Tessera comprises more than a thousand dark-matter-only zoom resimulations, each with a different cosmology and large-scale environment. Emulators trained on the suite predict the halo and galaxy populations of gigaparsec volumes at a resolution that a single periodic box of that size cannot reach.

Method

Full description

Parents

A low-resolution particle-mesh volume is run for every point in a Sobol' design over cosmology. Each takes about ten minutes on one GPU and is retained only as a density field.

Regions

One sphere is selected per parent at a prescribed percentile of that parent's overdensity distribution, so that cosmology and environment are sampled jointly.

Zooms

Each region is resimulated with SWIFT at 512 times the mass resolution of the parent. The remainder of the box is retained at low resolution to preserve the large-scale tidal field.

Galaxies

Merger trees built from all 140 outputs are passed to the SHARK semi-analytic model, and Synthesizer forward-models the emission of the resulting galaxies.

Emulation

Conditional normalising flows model each statistic as a function of cosmology and environment. Integrating over the overdensity distribution of the parent gives the global prediction.

Context

Wide-field surveys such as Euclid, DESI, Rubin and Roman will measure billions of galaxies over gigaparsec volumes. Interpreting their clustering requires simulations that are large enough to contain the rare haloes hosting luminous red galaxies and quasars, resolved enough to follow the lower-mass haloes hosting emission-line galaxies, and numerous enough to span cosmological and galaxy-formation parameter space.

No existing periodic suite satisfies all three requirements. Tessera instead combines many small, high-resolution regions, with the combination learned by emulation rather than simulated directly. The cost per cosmology is comparable to that of a standard-resolution Quijote box, while the resolved halo mass is three orders of magnitude lower.
