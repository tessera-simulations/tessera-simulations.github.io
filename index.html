Gigaparsec volumes, one tile at a time.

Tessera is a suite of more than a thousand dark-matter zoom simulations. Each samples its own cosmology and environment. Together, through emulation, they predict the halo and galaxy population of gigaparsec volumes at a resolution no single box can afford.

How it works

Read the full description

Parents

A low-resolution particle-mesh volume is run for every point in a Sobol' design over cosmology. Each takes about ten minutes on one GPU and is kept only as a density field.

Regions

One sphere is selected per parent at a prescribed percentile of that parent's overdensity distribution, so cosmology and environment are sampled jointly rather than on a grid.

Zooms

Each region is resimulated with SWIFT at 512 times the parent's mass resolution, retaining the full box at low resolution so the large-scale tides are preserved.

Galaxies

Merger trees from all 140 outputs feed the SHARK semi-analytic model, and Synthesizer forward-models the emission, so galaxies can be selected the way surveys select them.

Emulation

Conditional normalising flows learn each statistic as a function of cosmology and environment. Integrating over the parent's overdensity distribution recovers the global prediction.

Where Tessera sits

More results

Wide-field surveys such as Euclid, DESI, Rubin and Roman measure billions of galaxies over gigaparsec volumes. Interpreting their clustering needs simulations that are large enough to contain the rare haloes hosting luminous red galaxies and quasars, resolved enough to follow the lower-mass haloes hosting emission-line galaxies, and numerous enough to span cosmological and galaxy-formation parameter space.

No periodic suite does all three. Tessera trades a monolithic box for a mosaic of small, high-resolution regions whose combination is learned rather than simulated, at roughly the cost per cosmology of a standard-resolution Quijote box while resolving haloes three orders of magnitude less massive.
